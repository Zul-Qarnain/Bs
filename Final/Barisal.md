## II. Barisal Division

### Notes per Slide

*   **Introduction (Slide 3):**
    *   One of the **eight** administrative divisions of Bangladesh.
    *   Located on the bank of the **Kirtonkhola River**.
    *   Area: **13,225 km²**.
    *   Known as the **"Venice of the East"**.
    *   Previous name: **Chandradip**.
    *   Economy: Mostly farming and fishing.
    *   Part of **Sector 8** in the 1971 Liberation War.
*   **Location & Water Bodies (Slides 4-5):**
    *   Bounded by Dhaka (N), Bay of Bengal (S), Chittagong (E), Khulna (W).
    *   Crisscrossed by numerous rivers (e.g., Tentulia, Kirtankhola, Meghna) earning it the nickname "Dhan-Nodi-Khal, Ei tin-e Borishal" (rice, river and canal built Barisal).
*   **Administrative Structure (Slides 6-10):**
    *   Barisal Division formed on **1 January 1993**.
    *   Subdivided into **6 districts** and **42 upazilas**.
    *   Districts: Patuakhali, Pirojpur, Jhalokati, Barguna, Barisal, Bhola.
*   **Demography (Slides 11-13):**
    *   Population (2022 Census): **9,325,820**.
    *   **Least populous** Division in Bangladesh.
    *   Highest literacy rate (age 7+): **65.7%** (2011 Bangladesh census).
    *   Gender (2022): Males 48.8%, Females 51.2%.
    *   Religion (2022): Muslim (8,331,542), Hindu (750,154).
*   **Barisal City Corporation (Slides 14-15):**
    *   Administrative capital, on **Kirtankhola River**.
    *   Largest city and HQ of Barisal District/Division.
    *   Municipality established: **1876** (British rule).
    *   Upgraded to City Corporation: **25 July 2002**.
    *   Third-largest financial hub after Dhaka and Chittagong.
    *   Population (2022): **419,484**.
*   **Etymology & History (Slides 16-18):**
    *   Ancient name: **Bakla-Chandradwip**.
    *   **10th century:** Chandra dynasty established Chandradwip.
    *   **Early 13th century:** Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji led Muslim conquest.
    *   British created **Bakerganj district in 1797 AD**. Named after Agabak.
    *   District headquarters shifted to Girde Bandar (now Barisal city) in **1801**.
    *   Barisal division created on **1 January 1993** (from Bakerganj and Patuakhali).
    *   Name theories include "Borisalt" (from large salt deposits, British in **1757**).
*   **Educational Institutions (Slides 19-23):**
    *   High literacy rate.
    *   **Brojomohun College (B.M. College):** Established **1889** (school 1884), founded by Ashwini Kumar Dutta.
    *   **Sher-e-Bangla Medical College:** Started functioning in **1968** (foundation 1964). Named after A.K. Fazlul Huq.
    *   **University of Barishal (BU):** Public university established **2011**, academic activities began **24 January 2012**.
    *   **Barishal Zilla School (BZS):** First high school in Barisal Division, founded **23 December 1829**.
*   **Tourist Spots (Slides 25-31):**
    *   **Durga Sagar Dighi:** Largest dighi of southern Bangladesh (**2,500 hectare**), dug in **1780** by Rani Durgavati.
    *   **Kuakata Sea Beach:** "Sagar Kannya", **18 km long, 3 km wide**. Unique for visible sunrise and sunset. Rakhaines settlers came in **1780s**, dug "kua" (wells).
    *   **Floating Guava Market:** In Jhalakathi Sadar Upazila, Swarupkathi, Banaripara Upazila (Pirozpur). Hundred-year-old tradition.
    *   **Baitul Aman Jame Masjid Complex (Guthia Mosque):** Established **December 16, 2003**, 14 acres.
    *   **Jacob Watchtower:** Bhola district, **225-250 feet high, 16 stories**, tallest in South Asia.
    *   **Lakhutia:** Roy family estate, founded late 17th century. Destroyed in 1950 & 1971 riots/genocide.
    *   **Satla Beel ("Shapla Bil"):** Marshland famous for red water lilies (bloom Sep-Oct).
    *   **Barisal Divisional Museum** and **Sher-e-Bangla Memorial Museum**.
    *   **Char Kukri-Mukri Wildlife Sanctuary:** Bhola District, 8565 hectares forest, 217 hectares sanctuary.
    *   **Payra Seaport:** Patuakhali, important developing seaport for trade.
*   **Food and Festivals (Slides 41-42):**
    *   Barisal is a rice-producing center, known for Balam rice.
    *   **Narkel Pete Chingri:** Shrimp cooked inside a whole coconut.
    *   **Molida:** Rustic cold dish of soaked rice, mashed pulses, coconut, jaggery.
    *   **Biskee:** Home-based rice cake/snack.
    *   **Shirgoja:** Deep-fried sweet pastry soaked in sugar syrup, traditional from rural Bengal.
*   **Notable Personalities (Slides 33-40):**
    *   **A.K. Fazlul Huq (1873-1962):** "Sher-e-Bangla", statesman, Chief Minister of undivided Bengal (1937-1943), East Bengal (1954), Governor East Pakistan (1956-58). Born: **26 October 1873**.
    *   **Mohammad Mustafa Kamal (1947-1971):** Birshreshtha, sepoy in Bangladesh Army. Born: **16 December 1947** (Bhola).
    *   **Captain Mohiuddin Jahangir (1949-1971):** Bir Sreshtho, Bangladesh Army officer, Sector 7. Born: **7 March 1949** (Barisal). "Shaheed Jahangir Gate" in Dhaka named after him.
    *   **Jibanananda Das (1899-1954):** "Rupashi Banglar Kabi", famous Bengali poet.
    *   **Kusumkumari Das:** Poet, mother of Jibanananda Das.
    *   **Altaf Mahmud (1933-1971):** Musician, language activist, freedom fighter. Composer of "Amar Bhaier Rokte Rangano". Born: **23 December 1933** (Barisal).
    *   **Abdul Gaffar Choudhury:** Wrote lyrics to "Amar Bhaier Rokte Rangano".
    *   **Ahsan Habib (1917-1985):** Poet, literary figure. Born: **2 January 1917** (Pirojpur).
    *   **Ashwinikumar Dutta (1856-1923):** Social reformer, philanthropist. Founded **Brajamohan School (1884)**. Involved in Swadeshi/Boycott Movements, Swadesh Bandhab Samiti (banned 1908).

### Memory Techniques

*   **Barisal Division Formation:** **"93"** sounds like "new three", referring to **1993**.
*   **Barisal City Corp. Upgrade:** **"02"** for 2002 (sounds like "ooh-two, a new city").
*   **Highest Literacy:** Barisal, think of "Books and Rivers in **B**arisal" for high literacy.
*   **Kuakata:** **"Kua"** means well, remember the Rakhaines dug wells in the **1780s**.
