**Slide 2: Culture and Society in Bangladesh (Overview)**
*   Culture: Way of life, evolved over centuries, diverse social groups.
*   Manifestations: Music, dance, drama, arts, crafts, folklore, literature, festivals, cuisine, indigenous cultures.

**Slide 3: Outline of topics**

**Slide 4: The Culture of Bangladesh (Repeats Slide 2 content)**
*   Emphasizes indigenous communities' unique, colorful cultures.

**Slide 5: Linguistic Affiliation**
*   Primary language: Bangla (Bengali), Indo-European, spoken by ~300 million.
*   Regional variations (dialect differences in accent, grammar).
*   Forms:
    *   **Sadhu Vasha:** Learned/formal language (literature, essays, poetry).
    *   **Cholit Vasha:** Common/spoken vernacular.
*   Literacy: 50% men, 26% women (note: this data might be dated).
*   Minor usage/vocabulary differences between Muslims and Hindus.

**Slide 6: SYMBOLISM**
*   Most important symbol: Bangla language.
*   **Flag:** Dark green rectangle, red circle (left of center).
    *   Green: Trees and fields.
    *   Red: Rising sun, blood spilled in 1971 liberation war.
*   **National Anthem:** From a poem by Rabindranath Tagore, links love of nature/land with national identity.

**Slide 7: Urbanism, Architecture, and the Use of Space - Rural Architecture**
*   Houses: Commonly rectangular; dried mud, bamboo, or red brick; thatch roofs.
*   Interior: Little decoration, wall space for storage, minimal furniture (low stools).
*   Kitchen (ranna ghor): Separate small mud/bamboo structure; courtyard cooking in dry season.

**Slide 8: Urbanism, Architecture, and the Use of Space - Urban Architecture**
*   Styles: Moghul, Islamic motifs (curved arches, minarets), square British colonial construction.
*   **National Parliament building (Shongshad Bhabon):** Dhaka, by Louis Kahn; synthesis of western modernity and Islamic-influenced spaces.
*   **National Monument (Savar):** Wide-based spire, symbol of liberation.

**Slide 9: Literature (Part 1)**
*   Rich heritage, almost 1000 years old (Charyapadas as primitive Bangla).
*   Began with religious poetry/devotional songs.
*   Medieval literature: Mostly Theo-centric (Buddhist, Hindu, Muslim).

**Slide 10: Literature (Part 2)**
*   Medieval Muslim poets (Shah Muhammed Sageer, Alaol) introduced romanticism. Hindu poets used allegory (Vaishnava literature).
*   Medieval poets: Chandidas, Jayadeva, Vidyapati, Kasiram Das, Krittibas Ojha, Shah Muhammad Sagir, Daulat Kazi, etc.
*   Syncretic sects (Bauls, Sahajiyas) added distinct Bengali flavor.
*   19th Century: Humanistic Renaissance (influenced by English literature). Key figures: Akshaykumar Datta, Vidyasagar, Michael Madhusudan Dutt, Bankim Chandra, Rabindranath Tagore (introduced sonnets, novels, dramas).
*   Urban literature grew with education/print; folk traditions declined, later revived.
*   Rural essence remained central (Jibanananda Das, Jasimuddin).
*   Muslim writers (Kaikobad, Meer Mosharraf Hussain, Nazrul Islam) bridged community literary gap.

**Slide 11: FESTIVALS IN BANGLADESH**
*   Land of festivals. Popular saying: ‘Baro Mashe Tero Paban’ (13 festivals in 12 months).
*   Linked to religions, seasons, special occasions; international festivals added.
*   Grouped as: religious, non-religious, indigenous, non-indigenous.

**Slide 12: MAJOR FESTIVALS IN BANGLADESH (Context)**
*   Festivals common to all societies; nature changes with societal shifts.
*   Some deeply rooted, entertain generationally.
*   Primitive festivals (prayer for food) now colorful and varied.

**Slide 13: MAJOR FESTIVALS OF BANGLADESH (List)**
*   Pohela Boishakh, Pohela Falgun, Lalon Mela, Kite Flying Festival, Independence Day, International Mother Language Day, Nabanna, Ekushey Boi Mela, Dhaka International Trade Fair, Eid Ul Fitr, Eid Ul Adha, Muharram, Christmas, Durga Puja.

**Slide 14: POHELA BOISHAKH**
*   First day of Bangla year; celebrated nationwide.
*   Village celebrations: age-old customs, special meals.
*   Baisakhi melas.
*   Towns: Socio-cultural programs (e.g., Ramna Batamul - poems, songs, drama).
*   Traditions: Panta ilish in the morning, colorful dresses, buying sweets/toys at melas.

**Slide 15: POHELA FALGUN**
*   Welcomes spring (Bashanto); Falgun & Chaitra are spring months.
*   Celebrated February 13; called ‘Rituraj Bashonto’ (King of Seasons - Spring).
*   Attire: Reddish-yellow saris with red borders, flowers in hair (women); traditional pajama, punjabi (men). Face painting.

**Slide 16: LALON MELA**
*   Lalon song: spiritual anthem.
*   Five-day fair at Kushtia on Lalon Shah’s death anniversary ('Tirodhn Dibosh').
*   Held twice a year: Dol Purnima & 1st of Kartik.
*   Lalon Akhra (Mausoleum of Lalon Shah in Cheuriya, Kumarkhali Upazila, Kushtia) decorated; thousands of devotees attend.

**Slide 17: THE KITE FLYING FESTIVAL**
*   Shakrain Festival (Ghuri Utsob).
*   Annual Bengali celebration in Old Dhaka by Dhakaiya community.
*   Date: End of Poush (9th Bengali month) - Jan 14 or 15 (Gregorian). Known as Poush Sankranti.
*   Oldest annual festival; symbol of unity and friendship. Sky filled with colorful kites.

**Slide 18: INDEPENDENCE DAY**
*   March 26; biggest state festival; public holiday.
*   Dhaka: Booming guns herald day.
*   Floral wreaths at National Martyrs' Memorial, Savar by leaders, organizations, citizens.

**Slide 19: INTERNATIONAL MOTHER LANGUAGE DAY**
*   February 21; remembers martyrs of Language Movement of 1952 (to establish Bangla as state language).
*   Traditions: Black badges, barefoot walk to Shaheed Minar, offering flowers.

**Slide 20: NABANNA**
*   Literally "new food" (nava + anna).
*   Harvest festival celebrating new aman paddy crop.
*   Time of cultivation at beginning of autumn; granaries filled for winter.

**Slide 21: Ekushey Book Fair**
*   Amor Ekushey Grontho Mela ("Book Fair of the Immortal 21st") or Ekushey Boi Mela.
*   National book fair of Bangladesh.
*   Duration: Entire month of February.

**Slide 22: Dhaka International Trade Fair (DITF)**
*   International trade fair.
*   Organized by: Export Promotion Bureau & Ministry of Commerce.
*   Timing: First week of January to first week of February annually.

**Slide 23: Eid Ul Fitr**
*   Arabic: Eid (festival), fitr (to open/break fasting).
*   Three-day celebration on 1st day of Shawwal (10th Islamic month), after Ramadan.
*   Begins with sighting of new moon.
*   Characterized by: Eid prayers, time with loved ones, good food.
*   Celebrated with great éclat, new clothes, special food, sharing joy.

**Slide 24: Eid Ul Adha**
*   Feast of sacrifice; 10th day of Dhu al-Hijjah (last Islamic month).
*   Muslims sacrifice cow, goat, or sheep (reminder of Ibrahim’s obedience).
*   Major feature: Animal sacrifice, distribution of meat to poor/distressed.
*   Celebrated with religious fervor, new clothes, exchanging greetings, special foods.

**Slide 25: MUHARRAM**
*   First month of Islamic calendar; marks Islamic New Year.
*   Considered second most holy month after Ramzan.

**Slide 26: CHRISTMAS**
*   Known as "Bara Din" (The Big Day) in Bangla.
*   Celebrated in Dhaka and elsewhere.
*   Festivities: Illumination of churches, decorating Christmas tree.

**Slide 27: DURGA PUJA**
*   Biggest festival of Hindu community; continues for ten days.
*   Last three days most joyful; culminates with idol immersion.
*   Ceremonial awakening on 6th lunar day of Axwin. Puja on 7th, 8th, 9th; immersion on 10th.
*   Series of Pujas: Starts with Durga, ends with Saraswati (includes Lakshmi, Kali, Kartik).

**Slide 28: Buddha Purnima**
*   Main religious festival of Buddhist community.
*   Vaishakh full moon day commemorates three events in Buddha's life:
    *   Birth (623 BC)
    *   Enlightenment (588 BC)
    *   Mahaparinirvana (death, 543 BC)
*   Observances: Buddha Puja, Panchashil, Ashtashil, Sutra reading/listening, prayers, social/cultural/religious events.

**Slide 29: Bangladeshi cuisine (Overview)**
*   Vibrant culture, rich culinary traditions.
*   Influenced by geography, history, neighbors.
*   Known for rich, flavorful taste; emphasis on aromatic spices, fresh ingredients.
*   **Staple food:** Rice.
*   **Common meal:** Rice, fish, vegetables, lentils ("machhe-bhate-bangalee").
*   **Desserts:** Mouthwatering; sweets, rice-based puddings, milk-based delicacies (using rice, cow’s milk, jaggery/sugar).

**Slide 30: POPULAR BANGLADESHI DISHES**
*   **Kacchi Biryani:** Fragrant rice with meat, potatoes, spices.
*   **Bhuna Khichuri:** Hearty lentil and rice stew.
*   **Morog Polao:** Flavorful rice with chicken, onions, spices.
*   **Panta Ilish:** Breakfast dish; hilsa fish soaked in mustard oil and spices.

**Slide 31: SWEET TREATS OF BANGLADESH**
*   **Roshogolla:** Cottage cheese balls in sugar syrup.
*   **Mishti Doi:** Sweet yogurt (milk, sugar, cardamom).
*   **Jilapi:** Deep-fried dough spirals in sugar syrup.
*   **Rasmalai:** Sweet cheese dessert (milk, sugar, saffron).

**Slide 32: TRADITIONAL FOODS (Visuals)**
*   "মাছে ভাতে বাঙালি" (Bengalis are made of fish and rice).
*   "Panta Elish" in Pohela Boishakh.
*   "Pitha Puli" in Nobanno Uthshob.
*   "Vapa pitha, Chitoy Pitha" in Winter.
*   Friday Special Lunch.

**Slide 33: MUSIC IN BANGLADESH (Overview)**
*   Rich heritage, like literature.
*   Oldest form: Charyapadas. Medieval poetry composed as songs (raga/tala specified).
*   Kirtans: Blend of classical/folk; devotional songs by medieval Vaishnava singers.
*   Folk music: Most popular, timeless; nurtured by village bards. Rich in devotional mysticism, romanticism.
*   Well-known forms: Bhatiali, Baul, Marfati, Murshidi, Bhaoaiya, Gombhira.

**Slide 34: Some major genres of early Bengali Music**
*   Charjapaad, Nathgeeti, Geetagobindo, Sree Krishna Kirtaan, Boishnab Padaboli, Mongol Kabbo (Monosha Mongol, Chandi Mongol), Panchali, Dharma Mongol, Shakto Geeti, Tappa Gaan.

**Slide 35: Music (Categories)**
*   Main genres: Classical, Rabindra Sangeet, Nazrul Geeti, Folk songs, Adhunik gaan, Modern music (western influences).
*   **Classical:** Based on ragas (modes).
*   **Rabindra Sangeet:** Words are key; themes: prayer, love, seasons, patriotism, philosophy. Masterful poetry.
*   **Nazrul Geeti:** Works of Kazi Nazrul Islam (national poet, revolutionist). Themes: revolutionary, spiritual, philosophical.

**Slide 36: Folk Music (Part 1)**
*   Simple musical structure and words.
*   Historically, main entertainment for rural population. Many modernized into Adhunik sangeet.
*   Themes: Lifestyles (hermits, fishermen, cart drivers), worship of Bengali gods.
*   **Baul:** Most common category; by hermits, Sufism followers. Tradition of mystic spirituality.
    *   **Lalon Shah (1774–1890):** Prominent figure. Simple words, deeper meanings (creation, society, lifestyle, emotions). Vocal-centric.
*   **Lalon songs:** Most famous folk songs, subgenre of Baul; attributed to Lalon Fokir of Kushtia ("Baul Samrat").

**Slide 37: Folk Music (Part 2)**
*   **Bhatiyali:** Sung by boatmen; common in riverside areas.
*   **Bhawaiya:** North Bengal folk song (Rangpur, Cooch Bihar). Name from 'bhava' (emotion). Themes: love, spiritual ('fande pariya baga kande re').
*   **Jarigan:** Sung in groups (like Charya). Regions: Dhaka, Mymensingh, Sylhet, Faridpur.
*   **Hason Raja:** Devotional songs by Hason Raja (composer from Sylhet). Recently repopularized as dance music.

**Slide 38: Traditional Instruments**
*   Ektara, Dotara, Dhol/Dholakk, Khamak, Bansuri (flute), Shanai, Kartal, Dugdugi.
    *   Mainly used for folk music.

**Slide 39: Folk Art and Crafts in Bangladesh (Overview)**
*   Traditional arts/crafts evolving over time.
*   Distinction between art and craft often blurred (e.g., floor paintings/nakshi kantha as art; pottery/weaving as craft).
*   Some are combinations (e.g., painted earthen pots).

**Slide 40: Art and Crafts (Context)**
*   Folk art: Articles made by communities for religious, social, aesthetic needs.
*   Raw materials: Clay, wood, cloth, thread, shola (spongewood), conch-shell, reeds, bamboo, cane, horns. Gold jewellery not folk art, but copper/brass/silver ornaments can be.
*   Implements: Simple, even for village goldsmiths.
*   Gender roles: Women's folk art often non-commercial (alpana, nakshi kantha); men's commercial (blacksmiths, potters).

**Slide 41: Folk art motifs**
*   Traditional motifs reflecting land and people, repeated across forms.
*   Examples: Lotus, sun, tree-of-life, flowery creepers, fish, elephant, horse, peacock, swastika, circle, waves, temple, mosque.
*   Symbolism:
    *   Fish: Fertility
    *   Sheaf of paddy: Prosperity
    *   Lotus: Purity
    *   Swastika (curvilinear): Good fortune

**Slide 42: Folk art categories**
1.  **Painting:** Floor painting, stage scenery, painted pots, masks, wall painting, tattooing, chal chitra, karandichitra, fancy pots/dolls.
2.  **Embroidery:** Nakshi kantha, nakshi pakha, handkerchiefs.
3.  **Weaving:** Fancy mats, nakshi shika, fancy fans, baskets, flower vases.
4.  **Modelling:** Dolls, toys, utensils, masks, cake/sweet moulds, ornaments.
5.  **Engraving:** Wooden products, terracotta slabs, metal works.
6.  **Inlaying:** Ornaments with gems/pearls, decorating buildings with colored glass/stones.

**Slide 43: Alpana**
*   Popular folk art; Hindu women's religious vratas (Laksmivrata, Senjutivrata etc.), weddings, annaprashana.
*   Muslim weddings: Gaye halud (different motifs).
*   21st February: Floral/geometric patterns on roads to Shaheed Minar.
*   Motifs: Lotus, creepers (weddings). Abstract lines for vratas (e.g., Senjutivrata woman, Laksmi’s foot).
*   Material: White paste of pounded rice. Brick powder, ash, cow dung water, vermilion, turmeric also used.
*   Application: Finger, piece of wood (traditional); enamel paints, brushes (urban).

**Slide 47: Folk Art and Crafts (Materials - Part 1)**
*   **Bamboo craft:** Houses, farming/fishing/domestic implements, musical instruments (Ektara, flute). Decorated fences/partitions. Fancy fans (trees, birds, elephants).
*   **Cane craft:** Household/fancy products, furniture (chairs, stools, sofa sets, bookshelves). Nakshi Pati (pictorial mat) or Shitalpati (cool mat) still in demand.

**Slide 48: Folk Art and Crafts (Materials - Part 2)**
*   **Jute craft:** Hessian, carpets. Also mats, bags, sika (hanging string bags for food). Sika patterns: taraful, puntiful, takaful. Sika types: muthashika, chakshika, kadishika (threaded with cowries), nengtashika.
*   **Conchshell craft:** Bangles, armlets, rings, lockets, buttons, hairpins. Vermilion containers, incense holders, ashtrays, table lamps. Shankhari Patti (Dhaka) known for finely crafted articles.

**Slide 49: Folk Art and Crafts (Specific Crafts - Part 1)**
*   **Nakshi kantha:** Embroidered quilt; made/used everywhere. Rajshahi, Jessore, Faridpur most famous for stitchcraft/designs. Made from layers of old cloth. Uses: bedspreads, quilts, pillow cases, prayer mats, bags, book covers, food covers, dastarkhan (dining mats).
*   **Metalcraft:** Gold/silver ornaments, plates, glasses, pitchers, mugs (silver, bell metal) since ancient times. Also flower vases, ashtrays, table lamps. Engraved motifs: flowers, creepers, birds, geometric patterns. Gold/silver ornaments: makaramukhi mal (anklet), lockets (flowers, butterflies). Set with precious stones/gems/pearls.

**Slide 50: Folk Art and Crafts (Specific Crafts - Part 2)**
*   **Nakshi pitha:** Decorative cakes from pounded rice dough, shaped, fried, soaked in sugar syrup. Motifs: geometric, wheels, betel leaf, flowers, fish, birds. Popular in Dhaka, Mymensingh; for weddings.
*   **Ornamental woodwork:** Carved on doors, windows, pillars, chests, beds, musical instrument cases, palanquins, boats, dolls. Motifs: lotus, birds, creepers, circles.
*   **Sola craft:** Spongewood (from plant in paddy fields/water-bodies). Decorative articles: backdrops for deities, topar (bride/bridegroom headgear, often with kadam flowers), dolls, birds, elephants, flowers, boats, garlands.

**Slide 51: Folk Art and Crafts (Painting/Engraving)**
*   **Daruchitra:** Wood engraving art, ancient. Decorates door frames, pillars, windows, palanquins, boat prows, toys. Carved designs, painted plain surfaces.
*   **Dewalchitra:** Wall painting on mud houses (northern Bengal). Images of deities for protection; flowers/leaves for beautification. Motifs: swastika, Laksmi on lotus, Puranic heroes, paddy sheaves, peacocks, elephants.
*   **Gazir pat:** Scroll painting illustrating life of Gazi Pir (king of tigers). Panel form, depicts miracles (e.g., Gazi on tiger). Patuyas in Munshiganj, Narsingdi.

**Slide 52: Folk Art and Crafts (Masks, Scrolls, Pottery)**
*   **Painted masks:** Worn in folk festivals, religious dances (depicting deities like Shiva, Kali - blue face, red tongue).
*   **Patachitra:** Scroll paintings by Patuyas, illustrate narratives. Origin: Buddhist period (Maskari bhikkus showing Buddha stories). Later, Hindu Puranic stories.
*   **Pottery:** Inexpensive household utensils, Hindu god/goddess images, dolls, toys. Decorated (leaves, flowers, birds) or plain.
*   **Sakher handi:** Clay pots painted with fish, birds, lotus, leaves, geometric designs (bold strokes; red, yellow, green). Symbolic of fertility; used for wedding gifts. Rajshahi known for these.

**Slide 53: Family, kinship and marriage**
*   **Family/Kinship:** Central to social life. Bari (homestead) is core unit (economic, social, landholding). Rural: extended families, shared kitchen, sons under father's authority, wives under mother-in-law. Brothers separate after father's death, remain in same bari.
*   **Ties:** Patrilineal dominates, matrilineal crucial (women connect husband's family with own brothers).
*   **Women's role/status:** Inherit by Islamic law but often forgo claims to keep land patrilineal, ensuring place in brothers' homes. Respect/security grows after bearing a son. Mothers emotionally close to sons; daughters face stricter discipline, heavy chores. Father distant; son's wife may be stranger long after marriage.
*   **Marriage:** Primarily civil contract, not religious sacrament. Families key in spouse selection. Men (urban middle-class) may influence choice; women (except elite) little say. Between families of similar status; financial standing increasingly important (favors men with jobs abroad).
*   **Negotiations:** Extensive, balance status differences via finance. Groom’s family pledges bride-price (security for wife in divorce). Some follow Hindu dowry practice.

**Slide 54: Etiquette**
*   **Greetings:**
    *   Muslim: "Assalamu Walaikum" (peace be with you), response "Walaikum Assalam" (and with you).
    *   Hindu: "Nomoshkar" (hands together under chin).
*   **Interaction:** Men may shake hands (equal status, not firm grip); place right hand over heart after. Men and women don't shake hands. Same-sex conversation: touching common, close proximity. Closer status = closer interaction.
*   **Leave-taking:** "Khoda Hafez."
*   **Respect:** Titles or kinship terms for higher status (not personal names).
*   **Hospitality:** Visitors always offered a seat (stool/mat if no chair). Improper for visitor to sit on floor. Host must offer food.
*   **Public places:** Queuing not practiced (train stations, post office, bazaars); pushing common. Open staring not impolite.

**Slide 55: ETHNIC GROUPS IN BANGLADESH (Overview)**
*   For centuries, home to different ethnic groups.
*   ~35 smaller indigenous groups, ~2% of total population.
*   Location: Hilly zones, some plain land areas.

**Slide 56: The Indigenous People (Context)**
*   Mostly in Chittagong Hilly areas.
*   Defined as distinct (socially, economically, politically). Also known as Tribal People, Ethnic Group, Minorities.
*   Fascinating life/culture. Mostly Buddhists; some Christians, Hindus, Animists.
*   Primitive lifestyles seen. Women often do more hard work than men.

**Slide 57: Chakma**
*   Largest tribal community. Mixed origin, more Bengali influence.
*   Location: Chittagong Hilly Areas (Rangamati, Bandarban, Khagrachhari), Cox’s Bazar.
*   Origin: Mongoloid people from Southwestern Burma.
*   Language: Dialect (Arakanese – Indo-European), Bengali. Rich culture/tradition.
*   Housing: Traditionally bamboo; modern architecture also followed.
*   Livelihood: Agriculture, "Jhum cultivation" (eco-friendly).
*   Dress: Women - Pinon, Khadi (homemade); Men - Dhuti.
*   Script: Own script; ancient religious books in this language on palm leaves.
*   Religion: Vast majority Buddhists (largest Buddhist population in Bangladesh); older nature worship elements integrated.
*   Festival: **Bizu** (annual highlight, Chaitra - last Bengali month, 3 days long, public holiday in CHT).
*   Culture: More Southeast Asian than South Asian (no strict dietary restrictions/gender segregation like Bengali neighbors).

**Slide 58: Marma tribe**
*   Also Moghs or Maghs; second largest tribal group.
*   Race: Mongoloid.
*   Location: Chittagong Hills (Bandarban, Khagrachari, Rangamati), Cox’s Bazar, Patuakhali. Some in Tripura (India), Myanmar.
*   Origin: Burmese (Myanmar) ancestry. Culture, dress, script from pure Burmese.
*   Language: Arakanese dialect, written in Burmese characters. Urban Marmas speak corrupt local Chittagong language.
*   Religion: Animistic Buddhism.
*   Livelihood: Plow agriculture.
*   Dress: Men/women wear ‘thami’ (sarong), ‘angi’ (blouse); men's angi more a waistcoat. Make own dresses (traditional weaving) or buy Bengali dresses.
*   Culture: Unique; own dress, food, songs, instruments, beliefs.
*   Festival: **Sangrai** (biggest, New Year by Buddhist lunar calendar, Water Festival - splash water for purification).

**Slide 59: Tripura tribe**
*   Location: Mainly Khagrachari District (CHT). Also Comilla, Noakhali, Chandpur, Moulavibazar.
*   Population: ~1 lac in Bangladesh. Also in India.
*   Clans: 36 clans (dofas/sub-groups).
*   Language: **Kokborok** (spoken by >1 million).
*   Festival: **Boishu** (main festival).
*   Religion: Mostly Hindu; also own God. Some converting to Christianity.

**Slide 60: Santals tribe**
*   Oldest, largest Indigenous community.
*   Location: Rajshahi, Rangpur division. Also India, Myanmar, Nepal.
*   Language: **Santali** (Austroasiatic/Munda).
*   Origin myth: From 7 pairs of children of Peelchu Buro & Peelchu Buri; 7 groups (Parris/subgroups).
*   Culture: Rich. Religion: Sari Dharam, Sarnaism, Hinduism, Christianity. Supreme Deity: Marang Buru/Bonga.
*   Simple, love nature. Encouraging education. Love entertainment/festivals.
*   Festivals: **Sohrai** (principal), Baha, Karam, Dansai. Santal dance very popular.
*   Social: Patriarchy (son inherits property; daughter may get portion if father wishes).
*   Food: Rice, meat. Livelihood: Agriculture. Interested in art.

**Slide 61: Garos and Khasi Tribe**
*   **Garos:**
    *   Location: India (Meghalaya, Assam etc.). Bangladesh: Mymensingh, Netrokona, Jamalpur, Sherpur, Sylhet.
    *   Name: A·chik Mande (Hilly People).
    *   Religion: Mostly Christian; some Animist (Songsarek - tribal religion).
    *   Language: Garo (Tibeto-Burman family); >10 dialects by clan.
    *   Society: Matrilineal (take mother's title).
    *   Festival: **Wangala** (biggest, agricultural).
*   **Khasi:**
    *   Location: Khasi & Jaintia hills (India, Bangladesh - Rangpur, Mymensingh, Gazipur).
    *   Language: Khasi.
    *   Religion: Christianity; also tribal culture beliefs.

**Slide 62: Monipuri**
*   Ethnic group; original abode: Monipur state, Asam (India).
*   Location (BD): Sylhet, Moulovibazar, Habiganj.
*   Groups: 1. Bishnupriya Monipuri, 2. Moi Toi Monipuri. Languages named after groups.
*   Culture: Enriched; dance is part. Followers of old religion. Muslim group: 'Moi Toi Pangn’.
*   Food: Staple - rice, fish, dried fish, vegetables. Meat socially prohibited. Like 'sincheda' salad.
*   Occupation: Agriculture (crops, vegetables), home weaving. Entering all occupations.
*   Housing: Bamboo; also brick/tin shed. Neat and clean.
*   Festivals: Year-round merriments (dance, music, instruments). Rathajatra, Doljatra, Holi festival, Chaitra Sangkranti, Rashpurnima.

**Slide 63: Other Tribal People In Bangladesh**
*   Jaintia, Tanchangya, Khumi, Mro, Lushai, Khiang, Bawm, Pankhu, Chak, Rakhine, Keot (Kaibarta).
*   "Pangal" or "Pangan": Muslim tribal groups.
*   Other communities: Sadri, Chak.
