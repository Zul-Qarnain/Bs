## III. Khulna Division

### Notes per Slide

*   **Location (Slide 2):**
    *   Area: **22,284.22 sq km**.
    *   Location: South-western part of Bangladesh.
    *   Boundaries: Rajshahi, Natore, Pabna (N); Bay of Bengal (S); Rajbari, Faridpur, Gopalganj, Pirojpur, Barguna (E); West Bengal, India (W).
    *   Home to the **Sundarbans**, the world’s largest mangrove forest (southern Satkhira, Khulna, Bagerhat).
*   **Demographics (Slide 4):**
    *   Total population: **15,687,759**.
    *   Slightly more females than males.
    *   Majority Muslim (over 13.6 million), significant Hindu population (over 2 million).
*   **Administrative Structure (Slides 5-6):**
    *   **Second largest** of the eight divisions of Bangladesh.
    *   Headquarters and largest city: **Khulna city**.
    *   Comprises **10 Districts** and **59 Upazilas**.
    *   **Bagerhat** has the lowest population density; **Kushtia** has the highest density.
    *   **Narail** has the highest literacy rate (61.3%).
*   **Khulna City Corporation (KCC) (Slides 7-8):**
    *   Principal local government authority for Khulna city.
    *   Governed by an elected mayor and 41 councillors every five years.
    *   Functions under the **City Corporation Act, 2009**.
    *   Origins: Khulna Municipality established **1884**.
    *   Upgraded to municipal corporation in **1984**, later to City Corporation.
*   **Educational Institutes (Slides 9-11):**
    *   **Universities:** Khulna University (KU - 1991), Khulna University of Engineering and Technology (KUET), Khulna Agricultural University (KU), Jashore University of Science and Technology (JUST).
    *   Several notable colleges and schools including Khulna Collegiate Girls' School & KCC Women's College, Khulna Govt. Girls College, Khulna Zilla School.
*   **History (Slide 12):**
    *   **Ancient Period:** Part of Vanga, Gangaridai, Samatata kingdoms.
    *   **Medieval Era:** Ruled by Sena dynasty (12th C). **Khan Jahan Ali** (15th C) established settlements, renamed area **Jahanabad**.
    *   **Mughal Period:** Vikramaditya established a sovereign kingdom (16th C), defeated by Mughals in **1611**.
    *   **British Colonial Era:** British East India Company took control in **1793**. Khulna integrated into Jessore District in **1842**, became district HQ in **1882**, and municipality in **1884**.
    *   **Post-Partition:** Included in East Bengal (Pakistan) after 1947.
    *   **Administrative Formation:** Khulna Division established in **1960**.
    *   **Liberation War:** Strategic location, Sector 8.
*   **Tourist and Archaeological Sites (Slides 14-29):**
    *   **Sundarbans:** UNESCO World Heritage Site, largest contiguous mangrove forest (**~10,000 sq km, 60% in Bangladesh**). Home to Royal Bengal Tiger (est. **200-300 in BD**). Main trees: Sundari, Gewa.
    *   **Hiron Point (Nilkamal):** Good for wildlife spotting (tigers, deer, birds).
    *   **Dublar Char (Dubla Island):** Fishermen live Oct-Mar for dry fish. Site of Hindu festival **Rash Mela** (November).
    *   **Jamtola Beach, Kochikhali ("Tiger Point"), Kotka, Karamjal:** Other significant Sundarbans tourist spots.
    *   **The Port of Mongla:** Second largest and busiest seaport of Bangladesh (after Chittagong). Connected by rail to Khulna.
    *   **Shat Gumbud Mosque (Bagerhat):** Historic town founded by **Khan Jahan Ali (15th C)**. The mosque has **81 domes** (not 60), a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
    *   **Bagerhat Museum:** Built in **1995** (with UNESCO funds) to preserve Khan Jahan Ali's memory.
    *   **Khan Jahan Ali’s Tomb:** Significant pilgrimage site, Khan Jahan Ali died on **25th October 1459**. Part of a complex including Thakur Dighi.
    *   **KhanJhan Ali Bridge (Rupsa Bridge):** Connects Khulna city with southern districts and Mongla seaport.
    *   **Shilaidaha Rabindra Kuthibari (Kushtia):** Historic mansion, residence of Rabindranath Tagore (**1891-1901**). He started translating Gitanjali here in **1912** (Nobel Prize 1913).
    *   **Michael Madhusudan Dutt Memorial (Jessore):** Dedicated to the renowned poet.
    *   **Shahi Mosque (Jessore):** Mughal-era architecture (17th C).
    *   **Godkhali Flower Garden (Jessore):** "Flower Capital of Bangladesh", over 15 varieties of flowers.
*   **Food (Slides 30-31):**
    *   **Chui Jhal:** Fiery curry made with beef/goat meat and "chui" wild pepper, originated in Satkhira.
    *   **Prawn Brain Bhuna:** Shrimp brains cooked in spicy gravy.
    *   **Keora Dal:** Fragrant lentil dish with Keora fruit.
    *   **Hilsa:** National fish, prominent in Jessore (e.g., Shorshe Ilish).
    *   **Bhuna Khichuri:** Spicy rice and lentil dish.
    *   **Kachagolla:** Sweet delicacy of chhena and sugar syrup, popular during festivals.
    *   **Jessore Mangoes:** Langra, Gopalbhog.
*   **Eminent Personalities (Slides 33-36):**
    *   **Khan Jahan Ali:** Sufi saint, ruler over Jessore/Khulna region in 15th century. Died **25 October 1459**.
    *   **Michael Madhusudan Dutt:** (1824-1873) Bengali poet and playwright, pioneer of Bengali drama, "Father of the Bengali sonnet". Famous for *Meghnad Badh Kavya*. Born Sagardari, Jessore.
    *   **S.M. Sultan (1923-1994):** Renowned painter, known for depictions of muscular Bengali peasants. Born Narail.
    *   **Salauddin Lavlu:** Bangladeshi actor, screenwriter, and television director.

*   Khulna City Municipal Corporation upgraded: **1984**.

*   Khan Jahan Ali died: **25 October 1459**.
*   Bagerhat Museum built: **1995**.

*   **Michael Madhusudan Dutt** is known as the "Father of the Bengali sonnet."
