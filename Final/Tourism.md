## Presentation 1: Tourism Industry in Bangladesh

### Notes per Slide

**Slide 2: Introduction to Tourism**
*   Tourism is a fast-growing global industry, vital for foreign exchange and employment.
*   The word 'tour' comes from Latin 'tornus' (a tool for making a circle).
*   Definition: Movement of people from their usual residence to another (intending to return) for 24 hours to 6 months for leisure.

**Slide 3: Definitions of Tourism**
*   **WTO (1993):** "Tourism encompasses the activities of persons traveling and staying in places outside their usual environment for not more than one consecutive year for leisure, business, and other purposes."
*   **Rome Conference (1963):** Tourism is "a visit to a country other than one’s own or where one usually resides and works."

**Slide 4: Classification of Tourism (Part 1)**
*   Tourism classified into six categories by travel purpose:
    1.  **Recreational:** Leisure tourism away from daily life (e.g., hills, sea beaches).
    2.  **Cultural:** Satisfies cultural/intellectual curiosity (e.g., ancient monuments, historical/religious sites).
    3.  **Sports/Adventure:** Trips for activities like golf, hiking.
    4.  **Health:** Travel for medical treatment or health-giving places (e.g., hot springs, spa, yoga).

**Slide 5: Classification of Tourism (Part 2)**
    5.  **Convention Tourism:** Travel to attend conventions (business, profession, interest). Increasingly important.
    6.  **Incentive Tourism:** Holiday trips offered by companies as incentives for high sales performance. A new, expanding phenomenon (e.g., $3 billion business in the USA alone).

**Slide 6: Cox’s Bazar (Part 1)**
*   **World's Longest Beach:** 155 km unbroken natural sandy beach along the Bay of Bengal.
*   **Peaceful Beaches:** Soft, golden sand, gentle waves, stunning sunsets.
*   **Flourishing Tourist Hub:** Wide range of accommodations, restaurants, entertainment.
*   **Adventure Activities:** Water sports like surfing, parasailing, jet skiing.

**Slide 7: Cox’s Bazar (Part 2)**
*   **Natural Wonders:** Himchari National Park (forests, wildlife, waterfalls), Sonadia Island (wildlife preserve, sea turtle nesting ground).
*   **Family-Friendly Fun:** Many kid-friendly activities and attractions.

**Slide 8: SUNDARBAN (Part 1)**
*   **World's Largest Mangrove Forest:** Over 10,000 sq km, UNESCO World Heritage Site.
*   **Royal Bengal Tiger Habitat:** Estimated over 1,000 tigers.
*   **Diverse Wildlife:** Elephants, spotted deer, crocodiles, monkeys, snakes, 500+ bird species.

**Slide 9: SUNDARBAN (Part 2)**
*   **Birdwatching Paradise:** Over 350 bird species recorded.
*   **Thriving River Ecosystem:** Crisscrossed by rivers and waterways.
*   **Cultural Involvement:** Home to indigenous communities living in harmony with the forest.

**Slide 10: RANGAMATI**
*   Named for its red soil ("Ranga Mati").
*   Many attractive natural tourist places: Kaptai Lake (referred to as "capital lake"), hanging bridge, Shuvolong waterfalls (implied by "echo Village" possibly referring to natural acoustics or a specific spot).

**Slide 11: Kuakata Sea Beach**
*   Bird's-eye sea beach on Bangladesh's southernmost tip.
*   Located in Patuakhali district.
*   Unique view of both sunrise and sunset.
*   Distance: ~320 km south of Dhaka, ~70 km from Patuakhali district HQ.
*   Dimensions: 30 km long, 6 km wide.

**Slide 12: St. Martin's Island**
*   Small island (area: 8 km²) in the northeastern Bay of Bengal.
*   Location: ~9 km south of Cox's Bazar-Teknaf tip, southernmost part of Bangladesh.
*   Adjoining island: Chhera island (separated at high tide).
*   Proximity: ~8 km west of Myanmar's northwest coast, at Naf River's mouth.

**Slide 13: Patenga Beach**
*   Popular tourist spot near Bangladesh Naval Academy and Shah Amanat International Airport.
*   Beach width is narrow; swimming not recommended.
*   Part of seaboard built-up with concrete walls and stone blocks to prevent erosion.
*   Development in 1990s: restaurants, stands, improved lighting for night security.

**Slide 14: Jaflong**
*   Tourist hotspot in Sylhet, a hill station.
*   Location: Between tea gardens and hills, on Bangladesh-Meghalaya (India) border.
*   Home to the Khasi tribe.
*   Famed for lush tropical setting, natural beauty.
*   Attractions: Cascading waterfalls, greenery of Meghalaya mountains, historic Dawki Bridge (British-built suspension bridge, 1932).

**Slide 15: Ratargul Forest**
*   Only freshwater swamp forest in Bangladesh, maintained by the Forest Department.
*   Submerged for five to six months annually.
*   Attractions: Underwater trees, Ratargul Watch Tower, kayaking (3 people per kayak).
*   Wildlife: Various bird types and plant species.

**Slide 16: Bholaganj SadaPathor**
*   Location: Companiganj Upazila, near East Khasi Hills (Meghalaya, India).
*   Similar views to Jaflong.
*   Famous for: Beautiful stones ("heaven of white stones"), crystal clear water, green mountains.
*   Activities: Swimming, floating.
*   Nearby must-see: Utma Sora, Turong Sora.

**Slide 19: Advantages of Tourism in Bangladesh**

*   Infrastructure development.
*   Preservation of heritage sites.
*   Promotion of local products.
