**I. Social Structure: The Basics**

*   **Definition:** How units (people, groups) in a society relate to each other. Includes institutions, norms, values, roles.
*   **Building Blocks:**
    *   **Status:** Socially defined position (high to low).
        *   **Ascribed Status:** Involuntary, assigned at birth (e.g., female, prince, race).
        *   **Achieved Status:** Voluntary, earned through effort (e.g., doctor, student, spouse).
        *   *Mnemonic: **A**scribed = **A**ssigned; **A**chieved = **A**ccomplished.*
    *   **Roles:** Expected behaviors, obligations, privileges tied to a status (e.g., role of a student, role of a mother). Roles create predictability.
    *   **Social Institutions:** Organized systems of roles/norms meeting essential societal needs (e.g., Family, Religion, Education, Economy, Politics, Law). More formal in industrial societies, informal in tribal ones.

*   **Levels of Social Structure:**
    *   **Macro:** Large-scale forces; **Social Institutions** (Family, Economy, etc.) and their patterns.
    *   **Meso:** Mid-level; **Social Networks** connecting people, influenced by institutions & stratification (race, class).
    *   **Micro:** Everyday interactions; shaped by **Norms**, customs, and institutionalized ideas (race, gender).

*   **Types of Societies (Simple Progression):**
    *   **Hunting & Gathering:** Oldest, simple, nomadic, kinship-based.
    *   **Pastoral:** Domesticated animals, nomadic/trading, early inequality.
    *   **Horticultural:** Plant cultivation (gardening), more settled, complex division of labor.
    *   **Industrial:** Machine production, large populations, rapid change, science focus.
    *   **Post-Industrial:** Information/technology based, computers, knowledge is key.
    *   *Mnemonic: **H**unters **P**asture **H**orticulture **I**n **P**ost-Industry (HPHIP)*

**II. Social Stratification: Ranking & Inequality**

*   **Definition:** System of ranking categories of people in a **hierarchy**.
*   **Characteristics (Tumin):** *Mnemonic: SAUD C*
    *   **S**ocial: Based on social recognition, not just biology.
    *   **A**ncient: Existed even in simple societies.
    *   **U**niversal: Found everywhere.
    *   **D**iverse Forms: Varies across societies.
    *   **C**onsequential: Leads to unequal distribution of resources/opportunities.

*   **Principal Types of Stratification Systems:**
    *   **Caste:** Based on **ascription** (birth), rigid hierarchy, little mobility (closed system). Example: India's traditional Chaturvarna (Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Sudras).
    *   **Estate:** Feudal Europe, based on land ownership/duty (Clergy, Nobility, Commoners). Rigid, some mobility possible.
    *   **Social Class:** Based mainly on **economic position** (wealth, income, occupation). More **achievement-based** and open mobility than caste/estate.

*   **Theories of Class:**
    *   **Karl Marx:**
        *   Focus: Relationship to **Means of Production**.
        *   Two Main Classes: **Bourgeoisie** (owners) vs. **Proletariat** (workers).
        *   Prediction: Class polarization, conflict.
    *   **Max Weber:**
        *   Focus: Broader economic position, **Life Chances**.
        *   Components: Class (wealth/income), Status (prestige), Party (power).
        *   Recognized *diversity within* classes (e.g., skilled vs. unskilled workers).

*   **Class System in Bangladesh:**
    *   Post-1947 shift from Hindu to West Pakistani to Bangladeshi elite control.
    *   Traditional Muslim/Hindu distinctions (caste/lineage) less important now.
    *   **Wealth and Power** are primary determinants of class.
    *   Main Classes: Upper, Middle (can be subdivided), Lower.
    *   Emerging middle class has potential.

**III. Social Mobility: Movement in the Hierarchy**

*   **Definition:** Ability of individuals/groups to change their position within the social stratification system.
*   **Depends on System:** More mobility in open (achievement-based) systems, less in closed (ascription-based) systems.
*   **Types of Mobility:**
    *   **Horizontal:** Change position but **stay at the same level** (e.g., bus driver becomes truck driver).
    *   **Vertical:** Move **up or down** the hierarchy (e.g., promotion, demotion, rags-to-riches).
    *   **Intergenerational:** Change in status **between generations** (e.g., farmer's child becomes a doctor).
    *   **Intragenerational:** Change in status **within one person's lifetime/career** (e.g., starting as clerk, retiring as CEO).
    *   **Structural:** Mobility caused by **changes in the overall social structure** (e.g., decline of manufacturing jobs, rise of IT jobs affects whole groups).

*   **Social Mobility in Bangladesh:**
    *   System is relatively **open**.
    *   **Achieved status** (education, skills) increasingly important, though family/kinship still matter.
    *   Shift from traditional systems towards **meritocracy**, but not fully realized.

**IV. Rural vs. Urban Social Structure (Bangladesh)**

*   **Rural:**
    *   Classes: Capitalist farmers, rich/middle/marginal peasants, landless. Differentiation increasing.
    *   Power: Land, lineage, political links, *water lords*. Patron-client relationships.
    *   Values: More traditional, religious influence (Pir, Moulana), *samaj* (community spirit).
    *   Mobility: Households can move up/down economically over time.
    *   Lifestyle: Traditionally less differentiated, but education creating divisions (emerging *bhadralok*).
*   **Urban:**
    *   More modern, cosmopolitan (esp. big cities).
    *   Classes: Corporate execs, bureaucrats, professionals, industrialists, service workers.
    *   Status: Based more on **wealth and education**. Lineage less important.
