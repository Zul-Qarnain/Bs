# Rangpur Division Overview

### Notes per Slide

*   **Location (Slide 2):**
    *   Area: Approximately **16,185.01 square kilometers**.
    *   Location: **Northern part of Bangladesh**.
    *   Boundaries: West Bengal, India (N & W); Joypurhat, Bogra, Jamalpur (S); Assam, India (E).
    *   Main rivers: Brahmaputra, Jamuna, Punarbhaba, Karatoya, Atrai, Tista, Mahananda.
    *   **Tista Barrage Project:** Largest irrigation project in Bangladesh (615 meters long), located at Doani in Hatibandha Upazila.
*   **Administrative Structure (Slides 4-5):**
    *   Rangpur Division was formed on **25 January 2010**.
    *   Created by comprising **eight districts** and **fifty-eight upazilas** from the former Rajshahi Division.
    *   Comprises **8 Districts** and **58 Upazilas**.
    *   **Rangpur** district has the highest population (2,881,086) and density (1200/km²).
    *   **Panchagarh** has the lowest population (987,644) and density (703/km²).
    *   **Panchagarh** also has the highest literacy rate (51.8%) within the division, while **Kurigram** and **Gaibandha** have the lowest (42.5% and 42.8% respectively).
*   **Rangpur City Corporation (RpCC) (Slide 6):**
    *   Local governing body for Rangpur city and adjoining areas.
    *   Operates under the Ministry of Local Government, Rural Development and Co-operatives (LGRD) and **City Corporation Act, 2009**.
    *   **Second-largest** city corporation in Bangladesh by area (205.76 sq km).
    *   Population: Approximately **796,556**.
*   **Demographics (Slide 7):**
    *   Total population (2022 census): **17,610,956**.
    *   Population density: **1,088 persons/km²**.
    *   Majority Muslim (**86.51%**), followed by Hindus (**12.98%**).
    *   Largest ethnic group: Bengalis. Smaller indigenous communities: Santal, Munda, Oraon, Rajbanshi.
    *   2011 census population: 15,787,758 (density 980/km²).
*   **General Information & Education Sector (Slide 8):**
    *   Literacy Rate: **56.0875%** (overall division).
    *   Educational institutions include 1 government university, 1 private university, 396 colleges, 2,345 secondary schools, and numerous primary schools and madrasas.
*   **Origin of the Name (Slide 9):**
    *   Uncertain origin. Theories include:
        *   King Bhagadatta's "Rang Mahal" (palace of colors) during Mahabharata era.
        *   Payrabati (daughter of King Bhagadatta).
        *   "Rangrezpur" (dyeing factory for jute fabrics).
        *   Likely from Persian word "Rangpur," established during Ikhtiyar Uddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khilji's rule.
*   **History (Slides 10-11):**
    *   **Mughal Period:** Conquered by Raja Man Singh (Akbar's commander) in **1575**, fully integrated by **1686**. Mughal administration firmly established by **1611 AD**. Place names like Mughalbasa and Mughalhat signify Mughal presence.
    *   **British Period:** Came under British rule after East India Company obtained Diwani rights in **1765**.
        *   Fakir-Sannyasi Rebellion (Fakir Majnu Shah).
        *   Anti-colonial rebels: Devi Chaudhurani, Bhabani Pathak.
        *   Sepoy Mutiny (1857) impact.
        *   Civil Disobedience Movement (1930).
        *   **Tebhaga Movement** launched in **November 1946** (after meeting of peasant leaders).
    *   **Pakistani Period & Liberation War:** District of East Pakistan. Active participation in Bengali Language Movement, Six-Point Movement, 1969 Mass Uprising.
        *   First martyr (Rangpur) in Liberation War: Sangku Samajhder (**3 March 1971**).
        *   Armed resistance began: **28 March 1971** (attack on Rangpur Cantonment).
        *   Fell under **Sector 6** of Bangladesh Forces.
    *   **Post-Independence Period:** Former Greater Rangpur district divided into five districts in **1984**.
        *   Rangpur Municipality upgraded to City Corporation: **28 June 2012**.
        *   Rangpur declared **eighth metropolitan city of Bangladesh:** **16 September 2018**.
*   **Educational Institutes (Slides 13-16):**
    *   **Begum Rokeya University, Rangpur (BRUR):** Public research university, founded **2008**, named after Begum Rokeya.
    *   **Rangpur Zilla School:** Established **1832** as Rangpur Zamindar School. Government management since **1857**. Renamed **1862**.
    *   **Carmichael College:** Founded **1916**, known for academic excellence and cultural impact. Produced notable individuals like Jahanara Imam, Hussain Md. Ershad.
    *   Other medical, dental, and engineering colleges, along with numerous schools.
*   **Famous Tourist Places (Slides 18-24):**
    *   **Tajhat Palace (Tajhat Zamindar Bari):** Historical landmark, tied to Gopal Lal.
    *   **Kantanagar Temple (Kantaji Temple/Kantajew Temple):** Dinajpur, magnificent **18th-century** Hindu temple (Krishna/Rukmini), navaratna style, known for terracotta murals.
    *   **Nilsagar:** Large ancient pond in Nilphamari, excavated 8th century (King Dharmapal).
    *   **Ramsagar Dighi:** Large artificial lake near Dinajpur, excavated 18th century (Raja Ram Nath).
    *   **Bhitargarh:** Important archaeological site in Panchagarh, largest fortified ancient city (approx. **5th century AD**), served as trade center.
    *   **Teesta Barrage:** Lalmonirhat, largest irrigation project in Bangladesh, spans **615 meters**, 44 radial gates, completed **1997-98**.
*   **Traditional Foods (Slides 26-28):**
    *   **Shidol:** Fermented fish delicacy (dried fish, arum, spices), complex preparation.
    *   **Sholka:** Traditional spinach-based dish (jute leaves, taro stems, spices), unique chopping technique.
    *   **Palka Shalka:** Vegetable soup, made with various local greens. Often eaten with mashed potatoes and beef bhuna.
*   **Renowned Personalities (Slides 30-33):**
    *   **Roquiah Sakhawat Hossain (1880-1932):** Pioneer of women’s awakening. Founded Sakhawat Memorial Girls' School (**1909**), Anjuman-e-Khawatin-e-Islam (**1916**). Wrote *Abarodhbasini* and *Matichur*.
    *   **Abu Sadat Mohammad Sayem (1916-1997):** First Chief Justice of Bangladesh (1972-1975). President of Bangladesh after coups (1975).
    *   **Dr. M. A. Wazed Miah (1942-2009):** Reputed Bangladeshi nuclear scientist, writer, former Chairman of Bangladesh Atomic Energy Commission. Husband of Sheikh Hasina.
