## Measuring the Cost of Living – Consumer Price Index (CPI)

### Key Points:

*   **Consumer Price Index (CPI):**
    *   A measure of the overall cost of the goods and services bought by a typical consumer.
    *   Used to monitor changes in the cost of living over time.
    *   When CPI rises, a typical family needs to spend more to maintain the same standard of living.
*   **How CPI is Calculated:**
    1.  **Fix the Basket:** Identify a basket of goods and services bought by the typical consumer.
    2.  **Find the Prices:** Collect prices for each item in the basket at different times.
    3.  **Compute the Basket’s Cost:** Calculate the total cost of the basket at different times.
    4.  **Choose a Base Year and Compute the Index:**
        *   Designate a base year as a benchmark.
        *   **Formula:** CPI = (Cost of Basket in current year / Cost of basket in base year) * 100
        *   The CPI for the base year is always 100.
    5.  **Compute the Inflation Rate:**
        *   **Formula:** Inflation Rate = ((CPI in Year 2 - CPI in Year 1) / CPI in Year 1) * 100
*   **CPI vs. GDP Deflator - Key Differences:**
    1.  **Scope:**
        *   **GDP Deflator:** Measures prices of *all goods and services produced domestically*.
        *   **CPI:** Measures prices of *goods and services bought by consumers* (can include imports).
    2.  **Imports:**
        *   **GDP Deflator:** Excludes imported goods.
        *   **CPI:** Includes imported goods if they are part of the consumer basket.
    3.  **Basket:**
        *   **GDP Deflator:** Uses a *changing basket* of goods and services (reflects current production).
        *   **CPI:** Uses a *fixed basket* of goods and services.
*   **Problems in Measuring the Cost of Living (CPI Biases):**
    *   **Substitution Bias:**
        *   CPI uses a fixed basket, so it doesn't account for consumers substituting towards relatively cheaper goods.
        *   Leads to CPI *overstating* the increase in the cost of living.
    *   **Introduction of New Goods:**
        *   The fixed basket doesn't immediately reflect the increased purchasing power or variety from new products.
        *   New goods make each dollar more valuable, so CPI can *overstate* the cost of living.
    *   These biases mean CPI may overstate the true cost of living, impacting government programs that use CPI for adjustments.
*   **Nominal vs. Real Interest Rate:**
    *   **Nominal Interest Rate:** The reported interest rate, not corrected for inflation (e.g., rate a bank pays).
    *   **Real Interest Rate:** The nominal interest rate corrected for the effects of inflation.
    *   **Formula:** Real Interest Rate = Nominal Interest Rate – Inflation Rate
*   **Inflation:**
    *   A situation where the economy’s overall price level is rising.
    *   **Inflation Rate:** The percentage change in the price level from the previous period.
    *   Not all prices rise at the same rate; affects people differently.
    *   **Hyperinflation:** Extraordinarily high inflation (exceeds 50% per month). Example: Germany in the 1920s.
*   **Types of Inflation:**
    *   **Demand-Pull Inflation:** Results from excessive demand ("Too much money chases too few goods").
    *   **Cost-Push Inflation:** Originates from the supply side due to higher production costs.
*   **Effects of Inflation:**
    *   **Zero Inflation:** Stagnant economy, no incentive for increased production.
    *   **Mild Inflation:** Can incentivize producers, potentially leading to economic expansion.
    *   **High Inflation:** Can reduce demand for many products, lower production, and squeeze the economy.

### Potential Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs):

1.  **What is the Consumer Price Index (CPI) primarily used for?**
    *   A) To measure the total output of an economy.
    *   B) To monitor changes in the cost of living for a typical consumer.
    *   C) To determine the unemployment rate.
    *   D) To track changes in producer costs.
    *   **Answer: B**

2.  **Which of the following is the first step in calculating the CPI?**
    *   A) Find the prices of goods and services.
    *   B) Compute the basket's cost.
    *   C) Fix the basket of goods and services.
    *   D) Choose a base year.
    *   **Answer: C**

3.  **If the cost of the CPI basket was $200 in the base year and $220 in the current year, what is the CPI for the current year?**
    *   A) 90
    *   B) 100
    *   C) 110
    *   D) 120
    *   **Answer: C** (Calculation: (220/200) * 100 = 110)

4.  **The inflation rate is calculated as:**
    *   A) The absolute change in the CPI from the preceding period.
    *   B) The percentage change in the CPI from the preceding period.
    *   C) The CPI in the current year divided by the CPI in the base year.
    *   D) The nominal interest rate minus the real interest rate.
    *   **Answer: B**

5.  **Which statement best describes a key difference between the GDP deflator and the CPI?**
    *   A) The GDP deflator uses a fixed basket, while the CPI uses a changing basket.
    *   B) The CPI measures prices of all goods produced, while the GDP deflator measures prices of consumer goods.
    *   C) The GDP deflator includes only domestically produced goods, while the CPI can include imported consumer goods.
    *   D) The CPI is always higher than the GDP deflator.
    *   **Answer: C**

6.  **Substitution bias in the CPI refers to the problem that:**
    *   A) New goods are not introduced into the basket quickly enough.
    *   B) Consumers substitute towards goods that have become relatively less expensive, but the CPI uses a fixed basket.
    *   C) The quality of goods changes over time, which is hard to measure.
    *   D) Prices are collected from the wrong stores.
    *   **Answer: B**

7.  **How does the introduction of new goods typically affect the CPI as a measure of the cost of living?**
    *   A) It causes the CPI to understate the cost of living.
    *   B) It causes the CPI to overstate the cost of living.
    *   C) It has no predictable effect on the CPI.
    *   D) It makes the CPI a more accurate measure.
    *   **Answer: B**

8.  **If the nominal interest rate is 7% and the inflation rate is 3%, what is the real interest rate?**
    *   A) 10%
    *   B) 7%
    *   C) 4%
    *   D) 3%
    *   **Answer: C** (Calculation: 7% - 3% = 4%)

9.  **Inflation that exceeds 50% per month is known as:**
    *   A) Mild inflation
    *   B) Creeping inflation
    *   C) Stagflation
    *   D) Hyperinflation
    *   **Answer: D**

10. **"Too much money chases too few goods" is a common way to describe:**
    *   A) Cost-push inflation
    *   B) Demand-pull inflation
    *   C) Deflation
    *   D) Disinflation
    *   **Answer: B**

11. **Which type of inflation is caused by an increase in production costs?**
    *   A) Demand-pull inflation
    *   B) Hyperinflation
    *   C) Cost-push inflation
    *   D) Monetary inflation
    *   **Answer: C**

12. **According to the slides, what is a likely effect of mild inflation on the economy?**
    *   A) The economy stagnates.
    *   B) The economy squeezes as production falls.
    *   C) The economy expands as production increases.
    *   D) Consumers are not affected at all.
    *   **Answer: C**
