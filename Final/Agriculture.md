# Bangladesh Agriculture & Divisions Overview

## I. Agriculture of Bangladesh

### Notes per Slide

*   **Objectives (Slides 2-6):**
    *   **Historical Background:** Covers Ancient & Medieval (before 1757), Colonial (1757–1947), Post-Colonial & East Pakistan (1947–1971), and Post-Independence (1971–Present) eras.
    *   **Current Scenario:** Focuses on present practices, production, government policies, technological advancements, socio-economic factors, major crops, livestock, and farming techniques.
    *   **Challenges:** Addresses climate change impacts (flooding, salinity, drought), land degradation, small landholdings, water management, technology access, market volatility, food security, and policy constraints.
    *   **Interdisciplinary Approaches:** Explores how fields like climate science, engineering, economics, and social sciences contribute to agricultural improvement.
    *   **Climate-Smart Agriculture (CSA):** Defines CSA and discusses its potential, including technological, data-driven, and behavioral adaptations.
*   **Current Scenario & GDP (Slides 9-13):**
    *   Bangladesh ranks in the **Top 10 Globally in Agriculture** (2023).
    *   Agriculture remains the **main job generator** (Labour Force Survey 2022).
    *   GDP contribution data series provided from 1960–2023.
*   **Historical Background (Slides 14-17):**
    *   **Ancient & Medieval:** Farming dates back thousands of years (Indus Valley Civilization). Maurya & Gupta empires introduced irrigation. Mughal period saw agricultural expansion and new crops (potatoes, maize, tobacco).
    *   **Colonial Period (1757–1947):** British rule transformed agriculture with cash crops (indigo, jute, tea). The **Permanent Settlement Act of 1793** introduced the Zamindari system, leading to exploitation and famines, notably the **Bengal Famine of 1943**.
    *   **Post-Colonial & East Pakistan Era (1947–1971):** Agriculture remained backbone. **Green Revolution (1960s)** introduced HYV seeds, fertilizers, mechanization.
    *   **Post-Independence (1971–Present):** Initial food shortages led to modernization. Recent focus on climate-smart agriculture, organic farming, and digital technology.
*   **Challenges of Agriculture (Slides 22-29):**
    *   **Climate Change:** Bangladesh is highly vulnerable to floods, cyclones, droughts, rising temperatures, and salinity intrusion.
    *   **Land Degradation:** Issues include soil salinity, erosion, and overuse of agrochemicals.
    *   **Small Landholding:** Most farmers operate on small, fragmented plots (<1 hectare), hindering mechanization and investment.
    *   **Water Management:** Dependence on monsoon, inefficient irrigation (groundwater depletion for Boro rice), arsenic contamination.
    *   **Technology Access:** Limited adoption of modern tools and insufficient agricultural research/extension services.
    *   **Market & Price:** Poor market access, post-harvest losses, middlemen dominance, price volatility.
    *   **Food Security & Nutrition:** Malnutrition remains despite production gains, heavy reliance on rice limits dietary diversity.
    *   **Policy & Institutional:** Inconsistent policies, weak regulations, limited financial support for smallholders.
*   **Interdisciplinary Approaches (Slides 30-35):**
    *   **Definition:** Combining 2+ academic disciplines to solve complex problems.
    *   **Climate Science:** Climate models inform crop calendars; BMD & IRRI collaborate on heat/flood-resilient rice.
    *   **Engineering:** Develops solar irrigation, greenhouses; BRRI’s mechanized rice transplanting machine reduces labor by 50%.
    *   **Economics:** Models input-output, analyzes subsidies, evaluates crop diversification; BARC’s "Farm Economics Lab" studies high-value crops.
    *   **Social Sciences:** Assesses indigenous knowledge, gender roles, farmer adoption; participatory research in Rangpur improved pest management.
*   **Climate-Smart Agriculture (CSA) (Slides 36-40):**
    *   **Definition:** Integrated approach for cropland, livestock, forests, fisheries aiming to increase productivity/income, enhance resilience/adaptation, and reduce/remove GHG emissions.
    *   **Why in Bangladesh?:** Most climate-vulnerable country; agriculture accounts for ~13% GDP and employs 40%+ workforce.
    *   **Technological Innovations:** Stress-tolerant crop varieties (drought/salinity), Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) for rice (reduces methane), solar irrigation, remote sensing & GIS.
    *   **Data & ICT:** Agromet advisories via SMS/apps, digital dashboards, early warning systems.
    *   **Behavioral & Cultural Adaptation:** Tailoring CSA to local knowledge, incorporating indigenous climate indicators, community-based plans.
