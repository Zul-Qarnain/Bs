## Presentation 2: Major Archaeological sites and Tourist place in Bangladesh

### Notes per Slide

**Slide 1: Archaeological sites and Tourist place in Bangladesh**
*   Title slide.

**Slide 2: What is Archaeology**
*   Fundamental study of humanity and its past.
*   Studies things created, used, or changed by humans via material remains (e.g., stone tools, jewelry, pyramids).

**Slide 3: Definition by Society for American Archaeology**
*   "Archaeology is the study of the ancient and recent human past through material remains."
*   Scope: From million-year-old remains to 20th-century buildings.
*   Goal: Broad and comprehensive understanding of human culture.

**Slide 4: Types of Archaeology**
*   Diverse field; most archaeologists specialize by region or topic.
*   Specializations:
    *   Bioarchaeology (human remains)
    *   Zooarchaeology (animals)
    *   Ancient plants, stone tools
    *   Technologies for finding, mapping, analyzing sites
    *   Underwater archaeology (remains below water or on coasts)
*   Methods are similar globally; sometimes an independent field or part of historical research.

**Slide 5: The significance of Archaeological Sites**
*   Identity and belonging.
*   Historical perspective.
*   Tourism and economic growth.
*   Cultural diversity.
*   Documentation.
*   Education and awareness.
*   Legal protection.
*   International cooperation.

**Slide 6: Challenges to Archaeological Sites preservation**
*   Urbanization.
*   Climate change.
*   Conflict and war.
*   Lack of funding, resources.

**Slide 7: Archaeological Sites of Bangladesh**
*   Rich in archaeological wealth, especially medieval period (Muslim and pre-Muslim).
*   Much is unexplored and unknown due to difficult geography, climate, remoteness.
*   Post-1971 independence: Government undertook field projects, survey, exploration.

**Slide 8: Buddhist Patronage**
*   Discoveries add to knowledge of ancient Bangladesh history and culture.
*   Buddhism received royal patronage from dynasties like:
    *   Pala rulers
    *   Chandras
    *   Deva Kings
*   Led to numerous well-organized religious foundations.

**Slide 9: Lalbagh Fort**
*   Also known as Aurangabad Fort; a fine example of Mughal period architecture.
*   Represents an "unfulfilled dream of a Mughal Prince."
*   Location: Southwestern part of old Dhaka, overlooking Buriganga.
*   Plan: Four-sided, 1082' by 800'.
*   Key Structures: Small 3-domed mosque, grave of Bibi Pari (reputed daughter of Nawab Shaista Khan), Audience Hall of the Governor.
*   Purpose: Defensive enclosure for interior societies.

**Slide 10: Ahsan Manjil (The Pink Palace)**
*   Location: Kumartoli, Dhaka, on Buriganga bank.
*   Former residence and kachari of Nawabs of Dhaka; now a museum.
*   Construction: Begun 1859, completed 1872.
*   Named by Abdul Ghani after his son Khwaja Ahsanullah.
*   Known as Rang Mahal (newly built), Andar Mahal (earlier one).
*   Significant architectural monument.

**Slide 11: Sonargaon - the Old Capital of Bengal**
*   Location: Northern bank of Dhaleswari, near confluence with Sitalakshya, and Old Brahmaputra with Meghna.
*   ~27 km southeast of Dhaka.
*   Area now known as Panam in Sonargaon (2.5 km north of Dhaka-Chattogram highway).
*   Site of Bangladesh Folk-Art Museum (established by Shilpacharya Zainul Abedin). Museum hours: Fri-Wed, 10 am - 5 pm.

**Slide 12: Shat Gambuj Mosque**
*   UNESCO World Heritage Site.
*   Largest mosque in Bangladesh from the Sultanate period.
*   Described as "the most impressive Muslim monuments in the whole of the Indian subcontinent."
*   Locally known as 'Shat Gombuj Masjid' (Sixty Domed Mosque).
*   Actual domes: 77 over main hall.
*   Pillars: 60 stone pillars.

**Slide 13: Mainamati**
*   Once known as 'Samatata' (land even with sea-level).
*   Isolated 11-mile long Mainamati–Lalmai hill range in Comilla district.
*   Important Buddhist archaeological site.
*   Nearby: Comilla Cantonment, WWII cemetery.
*   Location: 114 km from Dhaka (2-hour drive towards Chattogram).

**Slide 14: Mainamati - Archaeological Significance**
*   Ruins of temples and viharas.
*   Artifacts from 7th-13th century AD.
*   Potential site of historical capitals.
*   Finds include: Fossilized wood tools, Hindu/Buddhist monuments, metallic coins, terracotta inscriptions, carved bricks, ancient ornaments, copper plates, seals.

**Slide 15: Paharpur Buddha Vihara**
*   Also known as Somapura Mahavihara.
*   Location: Paharpur, Badalgachhi Upazila, Naogaon District.
*   Among best-known Buddhist viharas in Indian Subcontinent.
*   One of Bangladesh's most important archaeological sites.
*   Designated UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.
*   Accessible via Jamalganj railway station; nearest airport: Sayedpur.

**Slide 16: Mahasthangarh**
*   One of the earliest urban archaeological sites in Bangladesh.
*   Location: Village Mahasthan, Shibganj thana, Bogra District.
*   Contains remains of ancient city Pundranagara (in Pundravardhana territory).
*   Brahmi inscription discovered in 1931 dates Mahasthangarh to at least 3rd century BC.
*   Fortified area in use until 18th century AD.

**Slide 17: Kantajew Temple (Dinajpur)**
*   Location: Near Dinajpur town.
*   Built in 1752 by Maharaja Pran Nath of Dinajpur.
*   Structure: 50' square, three-storied, on a raised sandstone platform.
*   Originally a nava-ratna (nine-towered) temple.
*   Towers collapsed in late 19th-century earthquake.

**Slide 18: Kantajew Temple - Architectural Highlights**
*   Originally Navaratna temple.
*   Extensively decorated surface with terracotta plaques.
*   Depicts: Hindu classic narratives, heavenly representations, contemporary social life, complex floral and geometric motifs.

**Slide 19: Wari-Bateshwar**
*   Site of ancient city dating back to 450 BC.
*   Location: Northeastern Bangladesh, Wari and Bateshwar villages, Belabo Upazila, Narsingdi District (75 km from Dhaka).
*   Significant discovery, challenges notions of early urban civilization in Bangladesh.
*   Discovered: Early 1930s by local school teacher Hanif Pathan.
*   Formal excavation: Started 2000, by Jahangirnagar University Archaeology Dept.
*   Believed to be "Sounagora," an ancient emporium mentioned by Ptolemy in *Geographia*.
